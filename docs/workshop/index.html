How to use this pack

  1. Read Part I first — it is the method, so you can check the method before accepting the numbers.
  2. Work through your own department's chapter. Every figure states its denominator; every claim is a count, a rate, or a date.
  3. Use the exercises at the end of the chapter. They are answerable from records the department already holds.
  4. Dispute anything you believe is wrong. The pipeline is open and re-runnable; a correction changes the number everywhere at once.

What this pack deliberately does not contain: no readiness index, no performance score, no ranking of departments, and no forecast. Those compress many facts into one number that then drives decisions. This pack keeps the facts and shows the arithmetic.

Part I

How this analysis is done

The method in six steps, so the dossier that follows can be reproduced, disputed, or run on another department.

  1. Fix the corpus. Every question asked in a sitting from 2021 onward is taken from the Assembly's own digital archive: 2,217 questions across 11 sittings. Nothing before 2021 is processed at all. The archive's own text is never rewritten — only normalised for matching.
  2. Classify each question twice. Once by policy domain (a hand-written codebook of ~18 themes with ~140 sub-clusters, each keyword assigned to the theme it actually describes; the department's sector breaks ties). Once by what the question demands: information, explanation, action or accountability. Every classification stores the keywords that fired, so any row can be re-checked by hand.
  3. Find the reply document. Each question points to a reply PDF. The text is read from the scan, with a mojibake detector that re-reads rather than stores garbage. Where no reply text can be recovered, the record is flagged with a reason class (refused, dateless, mixed page, blank) and listed for human review — never silently emptied.
  4. Code the reply against a rubric. Length band (brief, standard, detailed, very detailed), boilerplate share, self-contradiction, assurance and evasion signals, and three actionability flags: does it cite a timeline, a budget figure, and a responsible officer? The flags are what separate a long reply from an accountable one.
  5. Verify dates before using them. A reply latency is only computed from a submission/reply pair that passes the trust check. Pairs that fail stay in the data and out of the latency chart, so a timing figure is never built on a date pair that may be a scanning artefact.
  6. Count, never score. No index, no grade, no ranking, no forecast. Everything published is a count, a rate with its denominator printed, or a date.

Quality controls that run on every build

  • 160 automated tests over the parsers, classifiers and pipeline.
  • Extraction completeness: every scoped PDF accounted for; missing ones logged rather than skipped.
  • Two audits on the built artefacts: no overlapping labels in any figure, and no person named in a way that could match more than one member.
  • Numbers are computed at generation time — the report generator contains no hardcoded data values, which is checked.
Part I-A

Embedding audit: finding the same issue in new words

The semantic pass is an auditable supplement to exact matching, not a replacement for it.

The embedding pass represented 2,135 subject strings with nvidia/nemotron-3-embed-1b (2048 dimensions), then applied department, locality, named-scheme, specificity and verification gates. It produced 194 semantic families and sent 0 borderline pairs for human adjudication. Exact-match counts remain alongside these results so a reader can distinguish a measured wording difference from a model-assisted merge.

7. What this dossier does not show

  • Budget is one year against five years of questions. The rate in this dossier compares levels, not trends.
  • Question text is the portal's own text. Recurrence is grouped primarily with NVIDIA nemotron-3-embed-1b embeddings and verification gates, so paraphrased questions can be recognised as the same issue. Normalised exact matching remains an audit baseline for measuring merge differences.
  • Replies are OCR-extracted from scanned PDFs. Records flagged as needing human review are excluded from coded statistics but remain visible in the denominators shown here.
  • Reply length is a proxy for effort, not a measure of it. The rubric records length, boilerplate, self-contradiction and actionability separately, so a long reply that answers nothing cannot pass as thorough.
  • This dossier measures what the Assembly asked and what came back. It does not measure whether a department performed well, and it cannot: the assembly record does not contain that evidence.

8. Workshop exercises

  1. Answer the 38 action demands. For every question in this dossier classified as Action or Accountability, state the sanction or approval status, the executing officer, and the date of the next milestone. The rubric found a timeline in only 2% of 215 coded replies and a budget figure in 12% — these five fields are what turn a reply into a trackable commitment.
  2. Account for the 28 recurring subjects. Each was asked in two or more sittings. For each, state what changed between the first and the latest reply, and whether the change is documented or asserted. Recurrence without documented progress is the pattern this analysis is built to expose.
  3. Explain the 2 emerging subjects. These appear only in the last three sittings, in at least two questions and from at least two members. Which are new problems, and which are old problems asked in new words?
  4. Close the evidence gap. 6 of your 222 questions in this dossier have no readable reply text and are listed with reasons for human review. Either supply the missing document or state that no reply was tabled.
  5. Check your own numbers. Every figure in this dossier is reproducible from the public record: the question list, the reply PDFs, and the codebook. Re-run the pipeline on your own extracts before accepting or disputing a single number here — that is the point of the exercise.
  6. Adopt the standard. What would it take for every reply from this department to carry: a date, a responsible officer, a budget line where money is involved, and a statement of the next milestone? The cost is four fields; the effect is that the next question can ask about compliance instead of re-asking the original.

7. What this dossier does not show

  • Budget is one year against five years of questions. The rate in this dossier compares levels, not trends.
  • Question text is the portal's own text. Recurrence is grouped primarily with NVIDIA nemotron-3-embed-1b embeddings and verification gates, so paraphrased questions can be recognised as the same issue. Normalised exact matching remains an audit baseline for measuring merge differences.
  • Replies are OCR-extracted from scanned PDFs. Records flagged as needing human review are excluded from coded statistics but remain visible in the denominators shown here.
  • Reply length is a proxy for effort, not a measure of it. The rubric records length, boilerplate, self-contradiction and actionability separately, so a long reply that answers nothing cannot pass as thorough.
  • This dossier measures what the Assembly asked and what came back. It does not measure whether a department performed well, and it cannot: the assembly record does not contain that evidence.
